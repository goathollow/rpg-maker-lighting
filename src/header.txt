/*:
 * @target MZ
 * @plugindesc v{{VERSION}} Dynamic lighting: day and night, coloured lights, cones, beams, flicker, shadows and normal maps.
 * @author Goat Hollow Games
 * @url https://github.com/goathollow/rpg-maker-lighting
 *
 * @help {{NAME}}.js v{{VERSION}}
 *
 * Darkens maps and lights them with torches, lamps, crystals and more.
 * Everything is set up with notes and comments; no scripting needed.
 *
 * Keep the file named {{NAME}}.js: RPG Maker finds its parameters and
 * plugin commands by that name.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Quick start
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 * 1. Make the map dark. In the map's Note box (Map Properties), write:
 *
 *        <ambient: Night>
 *
 * 2. Give an event a light. In the event's Note box, write:
 *
 *        <light: Torch>
 *
 * Play-test, and the torch lights up the area around it.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Global light and map ambient
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 * The global light is the light level of the whole game world: daylight,
 * dusk, night. It starts at the "Global Light" parameter, changes with the
 * Set Global Light command, and stays changed across maps and in saves.
 *
 * Every map without an <ambient> tag follows the global light, so one
 * Set Global Light: Night darkens every outdoor map at once.
 *
 * Maps that should keep their own light, such as interiors and caves, get
 * an <ambient> tag in their note and ignore the global light:
 *
 *   <ambient: Cave>       a preset: Day, Evening, Night, Dungeon, Cave,
 *                         Darkness, or any you add in the parameters
 *   <ambient: #303860>    a colour, as hex
 *   <ambient: 48,56,96>   a colour, as red,green,blue (0-255 each)
 *   <ambient: off>        always fully lit, whatever the global light
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Lights by day and by night
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 * A light only shows where it is brighter than the light around it. As the
 * ambient rises to a light's brightness, the light fades out: torches
 * disappear in full daylight and come back at dusk on their own, with no
 * switches needed.
 *
 * A light's brightness at its centre is set by its intensity:
 *
 *   1      the map's own colours: shows in the dark, gone in full daylight
 *   1.25   a little brighter than drawn: a glow, faintly visible by day
 *   2      twice as bright (if Max Brightness allows)
 *
 * "Max Brightness" caps how far any light may brighten the map beyond the
 * colours it was drawn with: 1 = not at all, 1.25 by default, 2 at most.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Lights (event note or page comment)
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 *   <light: Torch>                    a preset
 *   <light: Torch radius: 240>        a preset with changes
 *   <light: color: #80ff80 radius: 100>   no preset, all settings given
 *   <light>                           a plain white light
 *
 * Settings you can change:
 *   color      #rrggbb or red,green,blue
 *   radius     how far the light reaches, in pixels (a tile is 48)
 *   intensity  brightness at the centre: 1 = the map's own colours,
 *              above 1 = brighter (see "Lights by day and by night")
 *   flicker    Fire, Candle, Pulse, Faulty or off (see "Flicker")
 *   shape      point, cone or beam (see "Cones and beams")
 *   shadows    on or off (see "Shadows")
 *   softness   how soft its shadows' edges are, 0-48
 *   height     how high it hangs, in pixels
 *   x, y       move the light from the event's centre, in pixels
 *
 * In the event's Note box, the light is on whenever the event is on the map.
 *
 * In a Comment on an event page, the light belongs to that page only. Use
 * this to switch lights with page conditions, for example:
 *
 *   Page 1                     Comment: <light: off>
 *   Page 2 (Self Switch A)     Comment: <light: Torch>
 *
 * A comment on the active page always wins over the event's note.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Cones and beams
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 *   <light: Flashlight>               a cone, pointing the way the event faces
 *   <light: Flashlight spread: 90>    a wider cone
 *   <light: Sunbeam>                  a beam, pointing down
 *   <light: Sunbeam direction: 45 width: 32 radius: 300>
 *
 *   shape      point (the default), cone or beam
 *   direction  facing: the way the event or player faces, turning smoothly
 *              as they turn. Or up, down, left, right, or an angle in
 *              degrees clockwise from up (90 = right, 180 = down)
 *   spread     a cone's width in degrees, 1-360
 *   width      a beam's width in pixels. A beam's radius is its length.
 *
 * Give the player a Flashlight (Set Player Light, or the Player Light
 * parameter) and it points wherever they walk.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Flicker
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 * Torches, candles, fireplaces and lanterns flicker, and Magic pulses. Any
 * light can be given a flicker preset, or have its flicker taken away:
 *
 *   <light: Lamp flicker: Faulty>     a lamp on the blink
 *   <light: Torch flicker: off>       a steady torch
 *
 * Flicker has two layers. Set them in the Flicker Presets parameter, or
 * change them in a tag:
 *
 *   slow       how far the overall level rises and falls (0-1): the long,
 *              slow breathing of a fire
 *   slowSpeed  how many times a second the overall level changes
 *   fast       how strong the quick pops and crackle are (0-1)
 *   fastSpeed  how many pops a second
 *   sharpness  0 = a soft shimmer, 1 = sudden snaps
 *
 *   <light: Torch fast: 0.3>          a Torch that crackles harder
 *
 * Every light flickers in its own way. To make lights flicker together,
 * give them the same seed:  <light: Torch seed: 7>
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Shadows
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 * Lights cast shadows with no setup:
 *
 *   walls        the tops of walls (A4) and roofs (A3) block light. Their
 *                edges facing a light catch it, a tile deep.
 *   characters   the player, followers, and events with character images
 *                cast shadows from their feet, away from each light, in
 *                the shape of the frame each one is showing. Their shadows
 *                fall on the ground and on the fronts of walls, never on
 *                the tops of walls, roofs or other tiles that block light.
 *
 * More tiles can block light. List region IDs in the Shadow Regions
 * parameter and paint them over crates, tables or trees, or list terrain
 * tags (Database > Tilesets) in Shadow Terrain Tags.
 *
 * Shadow edges are soft, and softer further from what casts them. A light's
 * softness is its size in pixels: 0 gives hard edges. A light's height sets
 * how long characters' shadows are: the higher it hangs, the shorter they
 * are. Characters are about 48 pixels tall.
 *
 *   <light: Torch softness: 24>       softer shadows
 *   <light: Lamp height: 200>         hung high: short shadows under people
 *   <light: Candle shadows: off>      casts no shadows
 *
 * A light on top of a wall lights the wall around it and shines out of it.
 *
 * Events (note or page comment, the page comment winning):
 *
 *   <shadow>                         cast a shadow. Objects (images whose
 *                                    names start with !) and tile images
 *                                    need this; characters cast one anyway
 *   <shadow: off>                    cast none
 *   <shadow: width: 32 height: 64>   stretch the shadow to this size, in
 *                                    pixels; normally it is the image's
 *
 * Maps (note):
 *
 *   <shadows: off>    no shadows on this map
 *   <shadows: on>     shadows on this map, even with the Shadows parameter off
 *
 * Shadows are the slowest part of lighting on phones. If a map with many
 * lights runs slowly, try <shadows: off> on it, or Light Quality: Quarter.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Normal maps and ambient occlusion
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 * Give characters, tilesets and parallaxes depth by saving these images
 * beside them, in the same folder, with the same name plus an ending:
 *
 *   Actor1.png            the image itself
 *   Actor1_normal.png     its normal map (or _n): which way each pixel faces
 *   Actor1_ao.png         its ambient occlusion map (or _occlusion, _o)
 *
 * Nothing else to set up: they are found by name. A tileset gets one map per
 * sheet (World_A1_normal.png, World_B_normal.png, ...). Each map is the same
 * size and layout as its image, frame for frame.
 *
 * Normal maps make light catch the sides of things facing it: bricks, folds
 * and faces light up toward a torch and fall dark away from it. Images
 * without one look exactly as before. A light's height (see "Shadows")
 * sets how low it shines across them: low lights rake across surfaces,
 * high ones light them more evenly.
 *
 * Normal maps are made "green up" (OpenGL style) by most tools, and that is
 * what the plugin expects. If light seems to come from below, set Normal Map
 * Green to "down".
 *
 * Ambient occlusion maps are greyscale: white is open, black is a deep
 * crease. Light from lights sinks less far into creases. Neither map changes
 * the ambient light (day and night, or a map's own): it lights every image
 * evenly, as without maps, so by day images look as drawn. Keep open areas
 * white: a map that is grey all over dims whatever a torch lights (or lower
 * AO Strength).
 *
 * Auto Normal Maps (parameter) gives images without a normal map a rough
 * one, worked out from their light and dark pixels. Try it on characters.
 *
 * After adding or removing these images, play-test once before deploying:
 * the play-test saves the list of them to data/GH_LightingMaps.json, which
 * browsers and phones need. When deploying, leave "Exclude unused files"
 * unticked, or they are left out.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Plugin commands
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 *   Set Global Light      fade the whole game's light level, e.g. to Night
 *   Set Map Ambient       fade just this map's ambient
 *   Reset Map Ambient     back to the map's <ambient>, or the global light
 *   Set Event Light       change or turn off an event's light
 *   Set Player Light      give the player a light (e.g. a Lantern), or "off"
 *
 * The global light and the player's light stay until changed again. Map
 * ambient and event light changes last until the player leaves the map; use
 * self switches and page comments for lights that should stay changed. All
 * of these are kept in save files.
 *
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 * Troubleshooting
 * ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 *
 * Press F8 during a play-test to open the console. Typos such as an unknown
 * preset name or an unreadable colour are reported there, with the map and
 * event they came from.
 *
 * The map isn't any darker: maps without an <ambient> tag follow the Global
 * Light parameter, which is blank (fully lit) to start with.
 *
 * A light doesn't show: by day, most lights are no brighter than the light
 * around them (see "Lights by day and by night"). Try <ambient: Night>.
 *
 * No lighting at all, and no errors: another plugin may build the map's
 * sprites its own way. Place {{NAME}} below it in the Plugin Manager.
 *
 * Lighting covers the map: tiles, the parallax and characters. Weather,
 * pictures, the timer, windows and battles are drawn as usual, unlit.
 *
